02 · The business

Know what The Company needs you to take over.

The Company needs one hands-on owner for a three-entity close, an outsourced-provider transition, and the move beyond QuickBooks Online.

  • A healthcare technology, services, and data business focused on psychiatry.
  • Supports more than 800 independent psychiatry practices across 49 US states.
  • Combines clinical software, billing and revenue-cycle services, and life-sciences projects.
  • A San Francisco-based Series B Public Benefit Corporation launching a new platform.
  • Three entities and several revenue streams create real close, intercompany, allocation, and revenue-recognition complexity.

Close and reporting

Own month-end, quarter-end, and year-end close across GL, AP, AR, and payroll for three entities. Deliver accurate US GAAP financials on a reliable timeline.

Multi-entity accounting

Own intercompany transactions, allocations, entity reporting, and the parent-level rollup across the MSO structure.

Technical accounting

Research and document positions, especially ASC 606 across software, revenue-cycle billing, and life-sciences projects.

Transition in-house

Take accounting back from the outsourced provider. Capture knowledge, close documentation gaps, and protect continuity.

Controls and process

Build policies, close procedures, review standards, and clear decision rights.

Systems, automation, and AI

Move from QuickBooks Online Premium to a true multi-entity platform. Redesign the chart of accounts. Use AI where it improves control and speed.

Business partnership

Give Shuchi and leadership clean numbers, clear analysis, and recommendations before they need to ask.

Build first, manage second

Do the work personally and operate with minimal support. Build the team only when the workload justifies it.

04 · Updated proof

Your résumé got you the call. These details can win it.

Lead with current scope: Mexico and US finance operations, direct reporting to a US-based VP of Finance, and a six-person team across both countries.

Walk through the close at transaction level: GL reconciliations, accruals, journal entries, balance-sheet support, variance explanations, and financial statements.

Use the NetSuite project as a decision story. You identified missed billable hours, showed the financial impact, earned approval, and led the migration.

Be precise about the five-month cutover and ten years of data. Explain why the project moved one month beyond plan and how you protected accuracy.

Connect your healthcare-software experience to the business model, but do not claim healthcare accounting or ASC 606 depth you cannot prove.

Use this answer frame

At [company], I reported to [title]. The team had [number] people. I personally owned [scope]. The problem was [risk]. I decided to [action]. The measurable result was [number]. Here is how I would apply that lesson at The Company.

06 · The interview

Questions Shuchi is likely to ask.

  1. 01

    Walk me through your current month-end close across Mexico and the United States.

  2. 02

    What does your team prepare, and what do you personally prepare, review, and approve?

  3. 03

    Tell me about the billing gap that led you to recommend NetSuite.

  4. 04

    How did you migrate ten years of QuickBooks data and prove the balances were complete?

  5. 05

    Why did the project take five months instead of four?

  6. 06

    How do you control intercompany transactions and cross-border balances?

  7. 07

    Tell me about a material variance you investigated and resolved.

  8. 08

    How would you take a three-entity close back from an outsourced provider?

  9. 09

    How would you assess ASC 606 across software, billing services, and project revenue?

  10. 10

    What accounting work would you automate first, and what would always require human approval?

  11. 11

    How would you operate during the first months with no six-person team?

  12. 12

    Why is this the right next level for you?

07 · Turn it around

Ask questions that sound like an owner.

  1. What part of the close is least predictable today?
  2. Where does the outsourced provider still hold knowledge that the internal team does not have?
  3. What close timeline would make you confident by month three?
  4. Which revenue stream creates the most accounting judgment or operating friction?
  5. What is already decided about the next accounting platform?
  6. Which control or reporting gap concerns you most right now?
  7. What decision rights will this role have during the transition?
  8. At the end of 90 days, what would make you say this was exactly the right hire?
